# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js 16 (App Router) with TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4, shadcn/ui on Radix, Recharts, lucide-react and next-themes, on Node 24. Runs locally at localhost:3000. Chosen by the owner.

## Users

One person, the owner, tracking their own money. No login, no other accounts, no hosting. Their first name is Mario; the Home greeting uses it, and they can change it themselves (a small box opened from the bottom of the sidebar).

## Product Purpose

A calm personal finance tracker for Indonesian Rupiah across the owner's accounts, starting with BCA, Bank Jago, Superbank and Pluang (more can be added). It shows balances, records income, spending and transfers, groups them by category, and tracks savings goals. It succeeds when checking in on money feels calm rather than stressful.

## Operating Context

Used mostly on a desktop or laptop, down to 1024px wide, and sometimes on a phone. The app is two parts on the owner's computer: `frontend/` (what they see) and `backend/` (a small server that saves everything in a SQLite file, `backend/data/nabungbego.db`). The data survives refreshes, restarts and other browsers. A phone on the same Wi-Fi can open the app through the laptop's address while the laptop is running. There is no login. It is not hosted anywhere.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- All UI text is in English.
- Money is Indonesian Rupiah, shown as "Rp 1.250.000": dot thousands separator, no decimals.
- Dates are shown as "29 Sep 2026".
- Income is green with "+", spending is red with "−", and transfers are neutral (owner's spec for the Transactions page, applied everywhere amounts are listed).
- Light and dark mode; the choice is remembered and defaults to the system setting.
- The keyboard can use everything: the first Tab stop on a page is a "Skip to main content" link, every control shows a focus ring when it has focus, dropdowns find an option by the letters typed so far (also in a production build, which `npm run build` makes without shrinking the code: the shrinking step deletes a few lines of the dropdown library), and a long unbroken word (a pasted link in a note) breaks at the edge instead of running off.
- Movement is small and only follows something done. When the system asks for less movement nothing runs on its own: no coin shower, no sweep, no flip.
- The total balance and each wallet's balance can be hidden with an eye icon, one toggle per card; the choice is remembered between visits.
- Pages: Home, Transactions, Wallets, Goals, Categories, Insights (and a friendly "Page not found" for any other address).
- Transactions can be added, edited and deleted, and wallet balances follow them. A transfer between the owner's own wallets moves money without counting as income or spending.
- **One-line quick add (expenses).** A round orange coin with a plus, bottom right on every page (no word on it; pointing at it or Tabbing to it shows a small "Quick add · Ctrl+K" label), opens a focused text box. `nasi goreng 25rb BCA` fills the existing transaction form for review; only **Add transaction** saves. It understands rb, k, ribu, jt, juta, whole rupiah and dot-separated thousands, plus decimal shorthand such as `1,5jt`. Full wallet names, or a wallet's optional nickname (a short word the owner sets, like `jago` for Bank Jago), match regardless of case; without a match it keeps the selected wallet and says so. **Ctrl+K** (Cmd+K on a Mac) opens it from any page, even while typing in a box; it is the only shortcut (a plain letter never opens it), and it doesn't open on top of another dialog, menu or list. The owner asked for the shortcut and nicknames on 7 Oct 2026, then chose Ctrl+K only. Unknown names stay in the note. Categories reuse earlier note suggestions and never replace a manual choice. Dates start as today and can be changed in the form. Multiple amounts or wallets, invalid or oversized amounts, and notes over 100 characters get a message without changing the draft. All parsing is local. The owner chose expenses only on 7 Oct 2026. The button began bottom left with a word on it; on the same day the owner moved it to the bottom right as a plain coin, because the sidebar felt crowded.
- Wallets can be added, renamed, given a note, a colour and an icon (each with a default) and an optional nickname for Quick add, put in any order, and deleted. Each has a type (bank account, digital bank, e-wallet, investment or cash), chosen when it is added. A wallet's starting balance is where it begins, not income. Opening a wallet (from Wallets or Home) shows its transactions.
- Deleting a wallet needs its name typed to confirm, and also deletes its transactions. The other wallets keep their balances. The last wallet, and a wallet a goal is saved in, can't be deleted (move the goal to another wallet first). For ten seconds after a wallet is deleted an Undo button in the message puts it back with everything that went with it (see "Undo after a delete" below).
- When a wallet's balance has drifted from the real account, the owner can correct it. The difference is recorded as a correction, never as income or spending, and every correction and value update can be looked up in the wallet's history.
- An investment wallet (Pluang) has its balance set by hand to its current market value ("Update value"). That changes the balance but is neither income nor spending, so it never appears in transactions or in the month's totals; the wallet shows how it moved since the last update, and the History shows a chart and list of every update.
- Goals (things the owner is saving toward) can be added, edited, deleted, put in the owner's own order and moved to another wallet. Each has a name, a target, what has been saved so far, the wallet it is saved in, an icon and, if wanted, a target date. A goal is a note about money already in a wallet, so "Add money" by default only updates the goal. It can also move the money: choosing another wallet as the source records a transfer into the goal's wallet (called "Saved for <goal>"), so goals and balances stay in step. Money can be taken out of a goal, for when it is spent. That only changes the goal and never moves a wallet. Each goal keeps a history of every change (started with, added, taken out, corrected by hand), with dates and a small chart. With a date, a goal says how much to save each month (whole months, rounded down, never fewer than one) and whether it is on pace or a little behind, in a calm way and never as an alarm. When money is added, a coin drops into the goal's bar. A goal that reaches its target is marked done, with the date it was reached, and moves into a folded "Completed" section; the moment is marked with a short shower of coins and paper and a message with a celengan in it. Taking money out of it, or raising its target, brings it back. Each wallet shows how much of it is set aside for its goals, and quietly flags a wallet whose goals add up to more than it holds. Deleting a goal leaves the money where it is.
- Categories group income and spending. Ten are presets that come with the app (Food, Transport, Bills, Shopping, Entertainment, Health and Other for spending; Salary, Bonus and Other for income): they can't be renamed, recoloured or deleted, but they can be moved, and a spending preset can have a budget. The owner can add their own (a name, a colour, an icon, and whether it is for spending or income), change a category of their own's name, colour and icon (never whether it is for spending or income, because its transactions would no longer fit), and delete it. A name can't repeat within the same kind, and can be 30 characters. Deleting a category moves its transactions to "Other" of the same kind, so nothing is lost and no wallet balance changes. For about ten seconds after a delete, an Undo button in the message puts it all back: the category with its budget and its place in the list, and its transactions (unless one of them has been changed since, or its name has been taken, and then nothing is touched and the owner is told). Spending and income categories can each be put in the owner's own order, which every list of categories follows.
- The Categories page is for looking after the categories: add, change, reorder, delete, and set budgets. Each category shows what was spent or earned in it this month (the month Home shows), and each row links to that category's transactions for the month, so the list adds up to the number clicked. Under the income categories, a "Needs a category" panel lists whatever is still filed under "Other" (the newest first, from any month) with a button on each to choose where it belongs: choosing files it at once, and the amount, date, note and wallet never change. It is only there while something is in "Other", and the transactions of a category that is deleted land there at once. It does not analyse: that is what Insights is for.
- The Insights page is for understanding where the money went. It has three views, chosen with a switch at the top, and what is chosen is kept in the address, so a refresh or the Back button lands on the same view. **Month** shows one month at a time (it starts on the month Home shows, and arrows or the chart step to any month from the first transaction up to this month): how much was spent, earned and left over, each said in plain words against the month before ("Rp 570.300 less than in August", never coloured as good or bad); a chart of up to 12 months that can show income next to spending, spending stacked by category, or the share of each month's income that was kept as a line; the spending categories ranked biggest first, with their share, how many transactions each was and how it compares with the month before; the biggest single expenses; the budgets, with the same calm bars as on the Categories page; the income categories the same way as spending; which wallets the spending came out of; what was spent on each day of the week, and the total balance day by day. Every category row links to its transactions for that month. **Compare** puts the month beside another one (the month before, the same month a year ago, or any month with transactions) as a table of totals and each category, with the difference in plain words. **Year** shows a whole year: spent, earned, left over and the average month, the twelve months in a chart and a table with a line for each, the year's spending and income by category against the year before, what a typical Monday, Tuesday and so on cost (averaged over the months with transactions), and the total balance through the year.
- "Export" on Insights saves a month, or a whole year, as a CSV file that opens in a spreadsheet: one line for each transaction with its date, type, category, wallet, note and amount (spending negative, income positive). The file is made on the owner's computer from what is on screen, and nothing is sent anywhere.
- Home links to Insights from its "Spending by category" panel ("See all"), and shows a "Budgets" panel under the month and spending panels, but only when at least one category has a budget.
- A spending category, presets included, can have a monthly budget: one amount, shown as a calm progress bar with the percentage used and what is left, or how far over it went. It is a guide: nothing stops spending from passing it, and it never raises an alarm. Income categories have no budget. The same bars are on the Categories page, on Insights and on Home. There is also one budget for the whole month, for everything spent together, next to the ones for categories: it is the first row of the Expense categories card on Categories (named "Whole month", and the only place it is set, changed or removed) and the first line of the Budgets panels on Insights and on Home. It follows the same rules: a guide, never an alarm, the same every month and set against the month shown. Its name leads to that month's spending in Transactions.
- When adding a transaction, typing a note suggests the category the owner used before for the same or a similar note. It only repeats their own choices, never replaces a category they picked, and is not offered when editing. The category list in the form ends with "Add new category", which opens a small dialog to add one without leaving the form.
- The **Total balance** line, on the Month and Year views of Insights, draws what all the wallets together held at the end of each day of the month (or year) shown, as one calm line: the figure at the end, how it compares with the first day in plain words that are never coloured ("Rp 12.254.800 more than on 1 Sep"), and the amount for any day on hover. Earlier days are worked out backwards from today's balances, using the transactions and the balances the owner set by hand (an investment's value updates and corrections), so it is only as accurate as those records. A transfer between wallets never moves it, it starts on the day of the first recorded change, and it stops at today. It follows the eye of the total balance: while that is hidden, the figure, the comparison and the line are hidden too.
- **Undo after a delete.** Deleting a transaction, a wallet or a goal can be undone: for about ten seconds the message has an Undo button (like the one after deleting a category) that puts back exactly what was there. A transaction comes back with the same effect on its wallet's balance. A goal comes back with its history, its pace, the date it was reached and its place in the list. A wallet comes back with its balance, its place, all its transactions (transfers with other wallets included, which never move those wallets' balances) and the balances set by hand for it. If something has changed in the meantime so that it no longer fits (a wallet or a category it needs has been deleted, or another wallet now has the wallet's name), nothing is touched and a message says why. Undo is remembered only while the app is running, for the last 50 deletes.
- The name the Home greeting uses can be changed by the owner: the bottom of the sidebar shows it with "Change name", which opens a small box with one field (at most 30 characters).
- **A wallet below zero.** Spending more than a wallet holds takes it below zero (correcting a balance by hand can't). The Total balance card (Home and Wallets) only counts wallets that hold money in its bar and percentages, so they always add up to 100%, and lists a wallet below zero apart under the legend ("Below zero: Bank Jago −Rp 2.684.500", with a small alert mark: calm, never an alarm). While that wallet's own balance is hidden, its line is left out.
- **A heads-up before it happens.** In the transaction form, an expense or a transfer out that would take its wallet below zero gets one quiet line under the amount: "This takes Bank Jago below zero. You can still save it." It never stops the save. Income, and the wallet a transfer arrives in, never get it; an edited transaction's old amount isn't counted twice (an edit that doesn't take more out than before says nothing); and it is left out while that wallet's balance is hidden, because it would give the balance away.
- **Messages that say which balance changed.** After a transaction is added, edited, deleted or brought back with Undo, and after money is added to a goal from another wallet, the message ends with where each wallet involved stands now ("BCA is now Rp 8.642.300."; a transfer names both wallets, and an edit that moved a transaction to another wallet names both). A wallet whose balance is hidden is named without its number ("BCA's balance is updated."). ("Correct balance" and "Update value" already said it.)
- **Goal milestones.** When "Add money" takes a goal past a quarter, half or three quarters of its target (by the percentage its card shows), the message carries a small celengan and says "A quarter of the way", "Halfway there" or "Three quarters of the way" (only the highest when one addition passes several, and never twice for the same mark). There is no confetti: that stays for reaching the goal.
- **The month in one look.** The Month view of Insights starts with a card, "September in one look" (with the year for a month of another year) and the round seal behind it: what came in, what went out, what was left over and the month's **biggest treat** (what it was, how much and the day). The biggest treat is the biggest single expense that isn't Bills, Transport, Health or Other, so the rent is never called a treat (a category the owner made counts as a treat); it is left out when there isn't one. A month with no money coming in or going out (transfers don't count) shows a calendar drawing and "Nothing came in or went out in October yet." ("yet" only for a month that isn't over), with a link to Transactions.
- **Twelve coins.** The Year view of Insights has a panel, "Months that kept money": twelve places for January to December, an orange coin for each month that kept money (more came in than went out) and a dashed empty ring for every other month: one with nothing in it, one that broke even or went over, one still to come. It counts nothing and never scolds.
- **Empty lists.** Opening Transactions from a wallet or a category (a wallet card, a category row, a link on Insights) that has nothing in it says so, instead of "Nothing matches these filters": "Nothing in Cash in drawer yet" with a wallet drawing and an "Add transaction" button, or "Nothing in Other in September 2026" (or "in 2026", or "yet") with a price tag and "Show every transaction". Any other mix of filters keeps the usual message. An address that isn't a page shows "Page not found" with the magnifier and a "Go to Home" button. (The Wallets page itself can never be empty, because the last wallet can't be deleted.)
- **Back up now.** A row at the bottom of the sidebar, under the owner's name: "Back up now", with "Last backup: 4 Oct, 15:30" (or "No backup yet") under it. Pressing it copies the data file into `backend/data/backups/`, named with the date and time (`nabungbego-2026-10-04-153012.db`; two in one second never replace each other), and a message says so. It only ever happens when it is pressed: nothing is backed up by itself, and old copies are never deleted. To go back to one, the owner stops the app, puts the current `nabungbego.db` aside, deletes the `-wal` and `-shm` files beside it and copies the backup in as `nabungbego.db` (the README has the steps). There is no Restore button.
- Assumption made during setup, not yet confirmed: transfers between the owner's own wallets are their own transaction type and count as neither income nor spending.

## Brand Commitments

- Name and logo: Kaya (written "Kaya", not all capitals), shown with the **Kaya mark**: four Parang-curved blades turning around a Kawung centre, with a clay diamond (the celengan) in the middle, drawn from the Kawung, Parang and Ceplok batik patterns ("four movements, one calm centre"). It replaces the round "K" badge, which is gone. The mark is forest green on light grounds and cream on dark ones; the diamond is its only clay, and the blades are never clay. Under 48px it is drawn without the seed holes. It isn't put inside a circle or coin, given a shadow, or recoloured, and it keeps clear space of at least a quarter of its width. The wordmark beside it is "Kaya" in Source Serif 4 SemiBold, letter-spaced 0.16em. The logo kit is in `kaya-logo/` and the app's copies are in `frontend/public/brand/`.
- The owner's style reference is binding: a calm, warm, premium "mindful money" feel, with a cream background, white cards, a deep forest green sidebar, sage and sand accents, and one small orange accent. Headings and big numbers use an elegant serif; body text uses a clean sans.
- Character: a **celengan**, the clay piggy bank people in Indonesia save coins in, is the app's one character. It appears on the empty Goals page and in the "Goal reached" message, with a notebook for an empty Transactions list, a magnifier for a search that finds nothing and for a page that isn't found, a wallet for a wallet with nothing in it, a calendar page for a month with nothing in it and a price tag for a category with nothing in it. It also appears, small, in the messages that mark a quarter, half or three quarters of a goal. Coins are the recurring shape (a milled rim on round badges, the orange Quick add coin, a coin that drops into a goal's bar; they are not the logo, which is the KAYA mark), and a hint of batik (the kawung lattice) is the texture, on the balance card and along the bottom of the sidebar. Fun lives in drawings and in the moments money moves, never in the way of reading a number, and never as an alarm.

## Evidence on Hand

Only synthetic mock data exists: 4 wallets, 13 categories (the 10 presets and 3 of the owner's own), 41 transactions from 1 Jul to 28 Sep 2026, and 3 goals. Merchant names in transaction descriptions are illustrative.

## Product Principles

1. Calm over alarm. Money out is shown in a muted clay, never a bright red, and there are no alarm banners or flashing states.
2. Numbers first. The amount is the clearest thing on any screen.
3. Local and private. The owner's data stays on their computer, in one file they can copy, and a button in the sidebar makes a copy for them.
4. One source of truth. Screens read typed data the backend sends, and the backend works out balances, so what is on screen never disagrees with what is saved.
